## @Noodle write up

> I have friends who work in cybersecurity in defense and FAANG and from what i remember on their resumes, a mix of these helped:
>
> - developing tooling for tasks such as scanning networks or reverse engineering related stuff (if you're into low-level hacking)
> - contributing to open source projects on existing tooling like wireshark and tcpdump (recommend getting familiar with the project's tech stack and programming for this)
> - participating and winning CTFs (helps to join a group or team up)
> - getting familiar with OWASP and web app sec principles and then pivot into bug bounties after enough practice (can earn money and credibility with companies this way)
> - Getting the CompTIA certs such as security+ and network+ on top of OSCP will guaranteed set you up for jobs as they're in demand all the time (requires a couple thousand bucks tho unfortunately and good to pair with existing experience as you go)
> - Ask and network your way into small IT/help desk jobs and get your hands dirty with networking/admin/active directory stuff, then pivot from there
>
> don't know what specific domain of cybersecurity you're into but these are just what i've picked up. there's always gonna be a high demand for red team/low level security jobs since companies are most affected in profit by malicious actors. lmk if you want any resources on learning stuff to compete in ctfs and bug bounties if that's your thing
>
> [...] i recommend at least picking up python and c and learning essential tools like wireshark and burp suite but again, not sure if you're into help desk related stuff or nitty gritty red teaming/vulnerability
>
> 1. I recommend going through the resources in this guide here for an overview on how to tackle different kinds of challenges in CTFs: <https://trailofbits.github.io/ctf/>
> 2. I've used this to force myself to pick up Linux shell scripting and commands which are highly useful for CTFs and for SOC-related jobs in general, most jobs will expect you to at least know your way around Unix environments so I recommend installing WSL2 if you're on Windows or use the cmd in Mac if you have an Apple laptop. And then complete at least half of the bandit box here if not more <https://overthewire.org/wargames/>
> 3. Really good free beginner CTF to exercise the skills learned above <https://picoctf.com/>
> 4. Highly recommend using TryHackMe as they got a lot of good beginner rooms and ctfs to teach you the essentials in cybersecurity. This one's a really good roadmap to follow specific rooms which teach you most of the essential tools you'll need to know <https://github.com/SUNNYSAINI01001/TryHackMe_Zero_To_Hero_Path>
> 5. Once you feel comfortable in CTFs and tools here's where the CTFs going on around the world get posted daily, you can usually participate solo if not in a team and work your way up the scoreboard <https://ctftime.org/>
